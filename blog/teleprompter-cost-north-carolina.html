Teleprompter Cost in North Carolina — Rates, What's Included, How to Book

7 min read • Updated November 2026

The first question on almost every call is the same: how much will this cost? Below is an honest breakdown of what we charge for teleprompter services across North Carolina—Charlotte, the Triangle, the Triad, and everywhere in between. Every job receives a written, itemized quote before you commit to anything.

North Carolina Teleprompter Rates

Service Typical range
Operator, half day (up to 4 hours) $600 – $800
Operator, full day (up to 10 hours) $1,200 – $1,500
Operator + full equipment package, including presidential glass $1,500 – $2,000 per day
Overtime past 10 hours Billed hourly, shown on the quote

These ranges cover most corporate events, galas, political appearances and production shoots in North Carolina. Your actual number depends on the factors below.

What Moves the Price

Equipment complexity

A single camera-mount prompter for a video shoot in a Wilmington studio is a simpler rig than presidential glass with confidence monitors on a ballroom stage at the Charlotte Convention Center. The more glass, monitors and cabling a job requires, the higher the equipment line on the quote.

Multi-day bookings

Three or more consecutive days earn 10% off the day rate. Multi-day conferences at venues like the Raleigh Convention Center or the Koury Convention Center in Greensboro often qualify.

Rush bookings

Jobs booked with less than 48 hours' notice carry a 50% rush surcharge. Two weeks' lead time avoids the premium entirely. During election season—when Raleigh fills up with campaign events—three to four weeks is safer.

Travel

A North Carolina–based operator handles NC jobs. Travel appears as its own line on the quote: IRS standard mileage round trip from the operator's base, plus a per diem when an overnight stay is required. It is never folded into the day rate, so you can see exactly what you are paying for.

What Every Booking Includes

  • A dedicated operator who loads, formats and scrolls your script live
  • Equipment setup and testing before doors open or cameras roll
  • A rehearsal pass with each speaker so they feel the pace before it counts
  • Real-time edits when the script changes five minutes before go-time
  • Direct coordination with your house AV team or production company

Whether the venue is a corporate boardroom in Research Triangle Park, a ballroom at the Omni Charlotte Hotel, or a stage at the Durham Performing Arts Center, the operator arrives early, confirms signal paths with the AV crew, and runs a full check before anyone steps to the lectern.

Venue and Logistics Notes for North Carolina

North Carolina spans major metro areas, mid-size cities and mountain or coastal event destinations. A few logistics details worth knowing:

  • Charlotte Convention Center / Spectrum Center area: downtown parking garages fill during large conventions. Confirm load-in dock access and timing with the venue's event services team in advance.
  • Raleigh Convention Center: the loading dock off Salisbury Street handles freight-elevator access to upper halls. Build in 30–45 minutes for equipment transport to the room.
  • Greensboro Coliseum Complex: multiple buildings, multiple docks. Confirm which hall and which dock your production company has reserved.
  • Pinehurst, Asheville and coastal resorts: travel time from the operator's base will add mileage and possibly an overnight. These are quoted transparently on a per-job basis.

At larger union venues, house rules may dictate who patches into the AV rack and when. We work in union rooms regularly and account for that in the timeline we send you before the event.

How Booking Works

  1. Send us the basics: date, venue, number of speakers, and the type of setup you need (camera-mount, presidential glass, confidence monitors, etc.).
  2. Receive an itemized quote: operator day rate, equipment, travel, and any overtime terms—each on its own line. Most quotes go out the same business day.
  3. Confirm with a 50% deposit. The deposit locks the date. The remaining balance is invoiced after the event.

Ways to Keep the Number Down

  • Book early. Avoiding rush pricing is the single easiest saving.
  • Bundle consecutive days. A three-day banking conference in Charlotte or a week-long shoot in Wilmington qualifies for the multi-day discount.
  • Send a near-final script ahead of time. Less reformatting on site means less billable time on the clock.
  • Tell us what your AV company already provides. If they are supplying monitors or a video switcher, we will not duplicate equipment you already have.
